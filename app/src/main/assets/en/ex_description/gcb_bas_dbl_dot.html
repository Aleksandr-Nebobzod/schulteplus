Stereo Dot

The exercise uses the space "Base", the main task here is to concentrate on an object with elements of virtuality, observing the indistinguishability of the nature of things for the mind.

Instructions

In this exercise, you must split the dots: gradually get four dots, then -- three. Physically, the eyes are looking as if into the distance[1], while attention gathers on the central dot and stays focused on it.

How to view stereograms...

Convergence, Divergence

Schulte for the youngest: Sacred Mountain

Our vision is stereoscopic, meaning each eye forms its own imprint of actuality. The fact that these imprints are not taken from the same points of view allows the brain to assemble a stereo image and model volume from it, calculate the distance to perceived objects, and create its own individual reality.
Moreover, each eye slightly turns depending on the distance — if the focus object is close, the eyes turn inward, which is called convergence (eyes are crossed), and if the object is far, they turn outward, and in full divergence, the eyes look parallel into infinity.

Stereo Image Perception Practice

You should calm your gaze, achieve a clear perception, and adjust the focus somewhere between the dots. Then, try to look at your nose (very gently and not "completely"), observing the behavior of the dot image according to the stages shown below.
Another option is to place a small object (pencil or finger, like in the story of Hurry-scurry) in front of the image, slightly below or above it. Look at the pencil, and use your peripheral vision to observe what the image looks like. Then, by moving the pencil closer or farther from you, achieve an image like in position 4 in the diagram:

  1. normal perception;
  2. slight convergence;
  3. excessive convergence;
  4. target convergence.

A similar technique is used when viewing stereograms. In the example below, you need to achieve such convergence that two marker dots turn into three (white or black, whichever is more convenient).
It can be noted here that the side dots seem to flicker, while the central one has a solid color and shape. After this, you should slowly and calmly shift your attention to the center of the image and wait. Your attention will naturally collect the hidden stereogram-object; once it stabilizes, you can shift your eye's focus to the object and examine it in more detail.

The given example looks better with convergence (cross-eyed viewing).

Stereo images where the distance between identical points is greater than the interpupillary distance of the viewer’s eyes are only suitable for cross-eyed viewing.


Steps from the instructions of the previous exercise can be applied. Additionally, during the session, it is important to be aware that:

  1. the central dot is a non-existent object;
  2. other meaningful contents are also non-existent objects;
  3. we keep perceiving only the dot...

Upon completing the exercise, press the left button in the "Base" space, and log your exercise result.
Realize that despite the illusory nature of the objects in this world, we live in this world, interact with it, and can be productive and happy.

Summary

The key outcomes of this exercise:

  • Increasing the level of abstraction of the perceived object.
Additionally, early acquaintance with illusions allows one to perceive reality more flexibly, to notice mental fluctuations more subtly, and with a light act of will, direct energy to what we consider beneficial for ourselves and others.
