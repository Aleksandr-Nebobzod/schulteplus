Pyramidot

The exercise uses the space "Base", and the task here is more complex than it was in the Stereo dot exercise:
-- control of the image plane position;
-- concentration on a complex object with elements of virtuality, plus mastering such an action as choice[*].
Depending on your conceptual choice (triangle or circle), your eyes will select the pyramid in the foreground or the moon in the background, creating the illusion of depth in the depicted space. (With parallel viewing, the moon appears in the foreground instead).
We remind you that our site provides an instruction for viewing stereograms, as well as the thematic story The Sacred Mountain in the "Schulte for kids" section.

Instructions

In this exercise, you need to split the images: gradually get four images, then -- three. Physically, your eyes will look as if into the distance[1].

  • Focus first collects at the central point (the moon) and holds on it,
  • then try to see the pyramid without shifting your focus.
  • Repeat the same with the triangle (keeping your gaze on it),
  • move attention to the clear perception of the circle (the moon).
Applicable steps from the previous exercise can be followed here. Additionally, during the exercise, be aware of the following illusions:

  1. the pyramid is closer than the moon;
  2. the moon is farther than the pyramid;
  3. the focus of vision and the meaning of the perceived figure must coincide.

Upon completion of the exercise, press the left button in the "Base" space, and fill in the journal entry.
Realize that despite the illusory nature of the objects in this world, we live in it, interact, and can be productive and happy.

Summary

Key results of this exercise:

  • eye relaxation when working with visual stimulus material;
  • managing the locus of attention independently[2] from the focus of vision.

[*] Choice is one of the key concepts in attention training. On the one hand, "freedom of choice" brings us closer to understanding free will. On the other hand, there is an opinion about the lack of freedom in any choice from limited options, but here, at its ultimate point, the unconditioned choice reveals itself as a creative act, which is no longer restricted by external boundaries, but uses these limitations as a resource for implementation.
[1] An alternative version of this exercise is possible with eye convergence, meaning the focus point of the eyes is closer than the stimulus material (the eyes are turned inward).
[2] Independence, or more precisely, "neuroplasticity" -- a significant property of consciousness that we aim to improve.
